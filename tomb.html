A story told through artwork

At the edge of a valley buried in ash, the Vicious Tribe kept watch beneath a sky that never seemed to brighten. The old paths were marked with stone and bone, and every shadow carried the memory of those who had crossed before. No map showed the way through. The tribe learned to read the wind, follow the distant lights, and trust the footprints left by its own.

     Each evening, a faint glow appeared beyond the mountains. The elders called it a warning; the young called it a promise. When the glow returned for the seventh night, a small band gathered its belongings and set out. They carried no banners and made no plans to return. Their only guide was a flicker on the horizon, vanishing whenever they drew near.

     The trail led through a forest of black branches, where the wind rattled like distant voices. One by one, the travelers found signs carved into the trunks: a circle, a split line, a pair of eyes. The marks matched patterns worn by the tribe, but none remembered making them. They followed anyway, leaving a mark of their own at every turn.

     Beyond the forest stood an abandoned gate, half-buried beneath the roots of a fallen tree. Its surface was covered in the same symbols, arranged like pieces of a story. The travelers searched for a key, a handle, or a gap in the stone. At last, the youngest member traced the marks in order, and the gate opened without a sound.

     On the other side, the mountain glow filled the valley. It was not a beacon or a hidden city, but a field of small lights drifting above the ground. Each light held a different shape, and together they formed a path leading toward the far ridge. The tribe paused, watching the lights move as if they were waiting for someone to follow.

     The travelers crossed the field slowly. Some lights faded when approached; others followed at a distance. In their shifting reflections, the tribe saw familiar faces, old journeys, and choices left unfinished. No two reflections were alike. By the time they reached the ridge, the lights had gathered behind them into a single, steady glow.

     From the ridge, the valley looked different. The dark paths connected; the scattered marks made a pattern. What had seemed like a warning was a record of every journey the tribe had taken. The elders recognized the first symbol, carved generations ago, and understood that the trail had not been leading away from home. It had been showing them how to find it again.

     When morning came, the glow disappeared. The travelers returned with no treasure and no grand discovery to claim, only a new story to share. They left the gate open and added their symbols to the old path. In time, others would find the marks, follow them through the dark, and decide for themselves what waited beyond the ridge.
